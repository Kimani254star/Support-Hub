About me

Independent developer, working directly with you.

I'm an independent web developer. I work directly with the people who own the business, so the person you explain the problem to is the person who builds the solution. There's no account manager in between and no hand-off to a team you've never met.

Most of my work starts with something a business already does on paper, in spreadsheets, or across three different apps that don't talk to each other. I turn that into one site or system that fits how you actually work, and I keep the interface plain enough that your staff can use it without training.

I build for the long run. A website is never really finished, because prices change, staff come and go, and customers ask for new things. That's why I stay on after launch to host, fix, and adjust what I built, and why this page has a support desk behind it.

What I do

Build it, launch it, keep it running

Three things, done by one person you can reach directly.

Websites

Marketing sites, booking pages, and storefronts, built to load fast, read well on a phone, and be easy to update yourself. I write the pages and the words around them with you, so the site sounds like your business.

Apps & dashboards

Internal tools and customer-facing apps, plus the admin panel to run them day to day. If you already keep records in spreadsheets, I can move them into something faster and safer.

Ongoing support

Once a site ships, I keep maintaining it: fixes, small changes, and a support desk clients can reach. You deal with me directly, and you get a straight answer about what a change will take.

How I work

From first conversation to long-term support

You always know what's done, what's next, and what it costs.

1

Talk it through

We begin with a conversation about what you sell, who your customers are, and what slows you down today. I'll ask a lot of questions at this stage, because a clear picture of your business saves weeks of rework later.

2

Build in small pieces

Then I build in small pieces and show you working versions along the way, so you can react to something real instead of a description. You'll always know what's done, what's next, and what it costs before I start on it.

3

Launch and support

After launch, you get one place to send problems and requests. Small fixes are quick, larger changes get a clear estimate first, and everything I build stays yours to keep and understand.
